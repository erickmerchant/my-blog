Recently I read
[I don’t know what to do about AI](https://davesmyth.com/existential-ai-crisis),
and while I have used LLMs, the post really resonated with me. It's prompted me
to think about where I stand with the technology.

First, LLMs cost money. I have no money, so I'm not using them. I have yet to do
the whole agentic coding thing. Historically I have avoided paying money for
anything, and never use services except a DNS provider basically. So sticking to
my frugal roots, I've started to look into
[local LLMs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_94KvCYnwQ) to explore agentic
coding. I have a few ideas that actually will require an LLM. Nice thing is I
really don't need the latest and greatest for them. That seems like a valuable
skill too, being able to use local LLMs. The sort of solution companies will
need once those Claude bills come rolling in. That's probably a one time trick
though, but it also gives me a story for job application questions. Right now I
lack an answer to "how have you been using AI".

Second, one thing has become clear to me recently. Holding onto skills in these
times, where LLMs have not fully shaken out, is essential. Even if I have a
project that uses an LLM, I can not go fully down that path and risk unlearning
core skills.

Anyways, there are no great insights here. That's just where I stand with the
technology. I'm not a super fan, but I also have some ideas and want to go about
it in a way that doesn't harm my wallet or my intelligence.
