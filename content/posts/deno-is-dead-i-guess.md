title = "Deno is dead I guess"
datePublished = "2026-10-10"
+++

After learning about [the news](https://deno.com/blog/cloudflare) today, I was
mad and saddened by it immediately in a way I don't think software news has ever
effected me. I sat on my couch for hours staring into space, questioning
everything. I eventually broke out of my dazed state, and walked my dog, going
over questions and a few possible answers in my mind as Dan, my dog, tried to
catch squirrels, but soon I was just enjoying the walk and not thinking about it
anymore. After not thinking about it, I finally made sense of the mess. And it
is a mess.

The biggest problem is that I've been using Deno Deploy, and the free tier is
quite good. And Deno Deploy is maybe the best hosting I've ever used. I can't
justify spending tokens or credits or whatever unit an alternative host is using
to hide the fact that if I got a lot of traffic I'd get hit with a large bill.
It took me all day to realize 6 months is a long time though and I can take time
to migrate things.

JSR is sticking around, but with no CSS support JSR was quite difficult to use
for frontend. Similarly deno bundle is great, but the differences with what it
can import and what Deno can have been annoying. I may be moving Handcraft and
Flint over to NPM, or I may abandon JS for a time.

The truth is I have been focusing on Typescript to become really proficient in
that language for jobs, but I am now. Proficient that is. Deno is great because
it allows you to run Typescript without really compiling it, which makes
learning way easier. Last year was the first time I actually completed the
Advent of Code and it was with Deno. I may go back to writing
[Rust](https://rust-lang.org/), my old favorite, or explore
[Gleam](https://gleam.run/), a language I've been eyeing for years. In the
immediate weeks I'm going to try to build a few game ideas with either
[Sycamore](https://sycamore.dev/) (a Rust wasm framework) or
[Lustre](https://lustre.hexdocs.pm/) (Gleam js framework). The biggest challenge
is going to be figuring out where Handcraft fits in my world if I end up liking
these alternatives.

So, I feel like I finally made sense of what to do, but I'm still mad. Maybe
more annoyed at this point. Like it was not entirely obvious to me that Deno was
not just an open source project that happened to have the support of a company.
To completely abandon it is bonkers to me. It will likely live on in some form,
but I'm likely moving on in 6 months or less.
